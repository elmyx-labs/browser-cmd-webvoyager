FINAL: My current top three by proximity are Proxima Centauri b, GJ 411 b, and GJ 887 e, with GJ 887 e sharing third-place distance with three other planets in its system.

“Top” is interpreted here as nearest confirmed super-Earths, using the broad mass range above Earth and up to about 10 Earth masses. This is a proximity ranking, not a claim about which planet is most habitable. Sources were checked on October 27, 2026; distance ties are broken by the lowest reported minimum mass.

1. Proxima Centauri b — about 4.2 light-years away. This super-Earth orbits the M-type star Proxima Centauri every 11.2 days. Its reported minimum mass is 1.055 Earth masses, and its discovery was announced in 2016. It is a particularly accessible example of a nearby, roughly Earth-mass world. [NASA planet profile](https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanet-catalog/proxima-centauri-b/)

2. GJ 411 b (Lalande 21185 b) — about 8.3 light-years away. Discovered in 2019, it has a reported minimum mass of 2.69 Earth masses and completes an orbit around its M-type host every 12.9 days, at about 0.079 AU. The research paper places the system 2.55 parsecs from us, consistent with approximately 8.3 light-years. [NASA planet profile](https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanet-catalog/gj-411-b/), [Hurt et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.09087v2)

3. GJ 887 e (Lacaille 9352 e) — about 10.7 light-years away. Announced in 2026, this super-Earth has a reported minimum mass of 1.46 Earth masses. It circles an M-type star every 4.4 days at 0.0417 AU. GJ 887 b, c, and d are equally close; e is selected because it has the lowest minimum mass among them. [NASA planet profile](https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanet-catalog/gj-887-e/)

All three were detected through radial velocity, so the quoted masses are minimum masses, not fully determined true masses.
