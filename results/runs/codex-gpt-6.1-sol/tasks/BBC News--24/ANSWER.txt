FINAL: The latest article on BBC News’ Space exploration page is “What is Nasa's Artemis III mission? And what comes next?”, dated 27 October 2026 and displayed as “9 hours ago” when checked.

Key points:
• Artemis III is planned for summer 2027. Four astronauts will test docking with Blue Origin and SpaceX prototype lunar landers in low Earth orbit.
• The mission is planned to last 14 days, potentially extending to 17, with three rocket launches and two dockings. The article explicitly says Orion’s return journey will test an upgraded heat shield during atmospheric re-entry.
• These rehearsals support Artemis IV, targeted for a 2028 Moon landing—the first crewed lunar landing since 1972.
• SpaceX’s unfinished lander and undemonstrated in-orbit refuelling threaten the timetable. BBC also reports that damage to Blue Origin’s only launch pad raises doubts about its ability to supply a lunar lander. NASA nevertheless expects the rebuilt pad to be ready by December or January 2027 for Artemis III’s summer launch.
• The wider goal is a sustained human presence on the Moon, with semi-permanent habitats envisaged by the mid-2030s and technology development for future Mars missions.
