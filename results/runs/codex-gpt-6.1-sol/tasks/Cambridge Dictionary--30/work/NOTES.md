FORM AND GENERAL USE
The form table gives have/has + the verb’s -ed form, with affirmative, negative, positive-question and negative-question rows. It supplies worked/worked? with the appropriate subject and auxiliary. The uses introduction connects events in the past with the present.

TIME + FOR AND SINCE
The article describes a present situation that started at a past point and continues now. Its comparison table assigns for to periods of time and since to a previous point in time. It uses the same house as an example: empty for three years versus empty since 2006. It explicitly rejects since with three years and for with 2006. The explanation says the house was empty in 2006 and remains empty at the example’s speaking time, 2011.

HOW LONG …?
The article says this question asks about the duration of a state or activity. Its worked-there dialogue answers with a starting date (21 August) and then a period (about four months).

YET
The article specifies mainly questions and negative statements, referring to things intended for the future that remain undone. Its unfinished-coffee example is explained as an intention to finish it; the negative homework question is explained as an intention to do the homework.

ALREADY
The article says this emphasises completion or achievement, often earlier than expected. Its examples concern booking a flight home and replying that teeth have already been cleaned.

STILL
The article says an expected event continues not to happen. Its examples concern an apology that has not been made and recovery from jet lag that has not happened.

INTRODUCING PAST TIME EVENTS
The article describes present perfect in newspaper headlines or TV news to report a recent event, followed by a series of past-simple verbs. Its news example first announces Heston’s death with present perfect, then supplies past-simple details about when and where he died and his life. It also describes introducing an open general point in present perfect, then using past simple for detailed specific information. Its second example first asks about seeing any Arthur Miller plays in present perfect, then identifies a particular production of The Crucible in past simple.

AMERICAN ENGLISH — ARTICLE’S SUPPORTING WORDING
“In American English the past simple is often used instead of the present perfect simple, often with already and yet.”
The comparison table pairs American past-simple questions about eating and finishing with British present-perfect questions. Yet accompanies the eating questions; already accompanies the finishing questions.

OTHER USES
The Experiences section describes experiences up to now without an important specific time. Recent completed events describes a finished event or state in the very recent past, without giving a specific time, often with just/recently. Past events, present results describes a single past action connected with the present, illustrated by a broken arm that is still broken and flowers that have not arrived and are not here.
