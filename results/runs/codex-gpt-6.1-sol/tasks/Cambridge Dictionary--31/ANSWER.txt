FINAL: Cambridge Dictionary’s English Grammar Today explains that could, may and might express different levels of possibility.

Its comparison gives these sentence examples:
- May: “The restaurant may close.” — presented as likely.
- Could: “The restaurant could close.” — presented as less likely.
- Might: “The restaurant might close.” — a possible outcome, with little certainty.

Cambridge cautions that native speakers differ about their relative certainty; stress and intonation also affect the meaning.
