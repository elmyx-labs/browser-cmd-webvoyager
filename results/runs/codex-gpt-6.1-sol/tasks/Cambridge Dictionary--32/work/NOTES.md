Exact wording: “We usually use less with uncountable nouns. We use fewer with plural nouns:”
Examples: “I do less work at weekends than I used to.” / “Better cycle routes would mean fewer cars and fewer accidents.”
The page says less is the comparative of little, fewer the comparative of few. It explains that less with plural countable nouns occurs in informal speech but traditionally is not considered correct. Before articles, demonstratives, possessives or pronouns, of is required: less of with singular nouns and fewer of with plurals. A noun can be omitted when its meaning is obvious.
