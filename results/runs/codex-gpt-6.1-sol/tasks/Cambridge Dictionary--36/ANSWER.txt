FINAL: Cambridge Dictionary’s English Grammar Today guide “Reported speech: indirect speech” provides the requested guidelines and direct-to-indirect examples.

Source: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/grammar/british-grammar/reported-speech-indirect-speech?q=indirect+speech
Accessed 27 October 2026.

Key guidelines:
• Statements: use a reporting clause followed by a clause with optional “that”.
• Yes/no questions: introduce the reported question with “if” or “whether” and use statement word order (subject + verb).
• Wh-questions: retain the wh-word, use statement word order, and omit the question mark.
• Commands: use a reporting verb with a to-infinitive; negative commands can use “not to”.
• Tenses often move back: present simple → past simple; present continuous → past continuous; present perfect and past simple → past perfect; will → would. Past perfect stays unchanged. Backshift is unnecessary when the information remains true or relevant, or the event has not happened yet.
• Adjust pronouns to match the speaker and listener. Adjust time/place expressions when needed: here → there; tomorrow → the next/following day; now → then.

Cambridge examples:
• ‘I’m tired,’ I said. → I told them (that) I was tired.
• ‘Are you ready?’ the nurse asked Joel. → The nurse asked Joel if/whether he was ready.
• ‘Who are you?’ she asked. → She asked me who I was.
• ‘Leave at once!’ they ordered. → They ordered us to leave at once.
