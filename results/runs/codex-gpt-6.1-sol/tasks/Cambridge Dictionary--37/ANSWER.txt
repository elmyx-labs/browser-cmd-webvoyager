FINAL: English articles distinguish an unspecified member of a class (a/an), an identifiable person or thing (the), and general reference that often needs no article.

According to Cambridge Dictionary’s English Grammar Today guide, “A/an and the”:

• A/an is the indefinite article. Use it with a singular countable noun when the listener or reader is not expected to know which one you mean. For example, “Do you have a car?” asks about any car. It is also used for jobs: someone can be a gardener or an ambulance driver.

• Choose a or an by the next sound, not the next written letter: a car, a university (starts with a /y/ sound), an apple, an hour (the h is silent). This also applies when an adjective comes between the article and noun, as in an excellent meal.

• The is the definite article. Use it when the intended reference is identifiable from shared knowledge or context. Asking where the car is parked assumes both people know which car. The can accompany singular countable nouns, plural countable nouns and uncountable nouns.

Countable and uncountable examples

                         Unspecified/general          Identified/specific
Singular countable       a lion                       the lion
Plural countable         books (books in general)     the books (a known set)
Uncountable              rice; nice weather           the rice from a named shop;
                                                      the weather last summer

• A/an cannot directly introduce a plural noun or an uncountable noun in its uncountable meaning. Do not say a nice weather. To count a quantity, add a unit or container: a litre of milk or a bowl of pasta. The article then belongs to the countable unit/container.

• Use no article for plural countable nouns and uncountable nouns when referring to them generally: books in general, or Japanese food in general. Add the when identifying a particular set, amount or instance.

Other useful patterns: the is used for universally known things such as the sun and often for musical instruments. Do not put an article before another determiner such as my or this: my job, this book.
