- Exact: “A/an and the are articles. They are a type of determiner and they go before a noun.”
- Exact: “The makes a noun specific.” A/an introduces a referent not already known; the identifies one already known.
- A vs an follows sound: a university / a one-year-old child; an hour / an MP3 player.
- Exact: “We only use a/an with singular countable nouns”; the can accompany singular and plural countable nouns.
- Exact: “We don’t use a/an before uncountable nouns”; specific uncountables can take the.
- Examples: “I have a sister and a brother.” “That was an excellent meal.” “The lion roared.” “The lions roared.”
- Uncountable examples: rice without an article for a general request; the rice bought in the Thai shop for a specified quantity; nice weather without a; the weather for a particular time.
- Quantities use a bit of, a piece of or a specific measure: a litre of milk, a big bowl of pasta.
- General plurals and general uncountables normally take no the: books in general, Japanese food in general.
- No article together with determiners such as my/this/some; use a/an for professions; the for universally known things, e.g. sun.
