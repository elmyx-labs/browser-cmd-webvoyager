FINAL: Sustainable Agricultural Land Management, offered by the University of Florida on Coursera, is a suitable course on sustainable agriculture practices.

Course: https://www.coursera.org/learn/sustainable-agriculture

The course's objectives are to:

- Understand agricultural and urban water-quality issues in Florida and their causes.
- Study land and nutrient management strategies, including the science and policy behind best management practices (BMPs).
- Evaluate BMP research and analyze how it informs practices and policies that protect water quality.
- Explore practical management topics through modules on soil management, nutrient management, agricultural water and irrigation management.

The course description and eight-module syllabus support these objectives. Its focus is sustainable land and water management, particularly Florida's water-quality challenges.

Lead instructor: George J. Hochmuth, Ph.D., the course's sole listed instructor. His University of Florida profile identifies him as Professor Emeritus in Plant Nutrition and Soil Fertility in the Department of Soil, Water, and Ecosystem Sciences.
Source: https://soils.ifas.ufl.edu/people/emeritus-faculty/george-j-hochmuth/

His Coursera biography describes research on urban and agricultural fertilization, water quality, irrigation efficiency and nutrient management, particularly nitrogen and phosphorus. His research questions address irrigation and fertilization practices that prevent nitrogen losses, and BMPs for retaining nutrients and water in the plant root zone. The biography explicitly asks: “What are the best management practices for keeping nutrients and water in the plant root zone?” It also examines crop rotation, cover crops and mulching as ways to minimize environmental nutrient losses.
Source: https://www.coursera.org/instructor/hochmuth

His career includes faculty work in Plant and Soil Sciences at the University of Massachusetts Amherst before joining the University of Florida in 1984. He led statewide vegetable nutrient and water-management research and extension programs: UF's announcement states that he “led statewide research and extension programs in vegetable nutrient and water management.” He also directed UF's North Florida Research and Education Center and was appointed UF/IFAS associate dean for research in 2006. He is a Fellow of the American Society for Horticultural Science.
Source: University career announcement, May 1, 2006: https://blogs.ifas.ufl.edu/news/2006/05/01/george-hochmuth-appointed-ufifas-associate-dean-for-research/
